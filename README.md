# Scheme Builders

**Scout your opponent. Build a defensive game plan. Understand your next call.**

Scheme Builders is a free defensive scouting and planning app for **EA SPORTS College Football 27**. It turns the offensive tendencies you observe into defensive formation, play, and adjustment recommendations, with explanations of your job and the risks behind each choice.

Built with user-versus-user games in mind, it also helps you explore defensive playbooks, learn formations, and design custom defensive setups. It runs as a standalone companion app; scouting inputs and in-game adjustments are entered manually.

**[Open the app](https://schemebuilders.com)** · **[Report an issue](https://github.com/ckeysvt88/schemebuilderv2/issues)**

Free to use. No ads. No account required. Works in your browser and can be added to your phone’s home screen.

## Getting started

1. **Scout the offense.** Choose the tendencies you have actually seen: personnel, run style, passing concepts, field targets, quarterback habits, and situations. Set the opponent’s run/pass tendency.
2. **Build your game plan.** Choose your defensive playbook, **My Defensive User**, and **Game Objective**.
3. **Set the current situation.** Update the offensive formation or personnel, down, and distance as the game changes.
4. **Choose a call.** Start with the primary in **Your call plan**. Review its setup, your job, and the trigger for changing calls. Formation cards also show **Best Overall** and **Best For You**.
5. **Review what happened.** Save opponent profiles, keep notes, or use **Test This Call** to record results from your game.

You can also start from **Teams** and adjust the supplied team profile to match how your actual opponent plays. Open **Guide** on Scout for the tutorial and its topic picker.

### Four ways to view your plan

Open **Layout**, to the right of **Adjust** on the lower Plan header row, to choose a view. **Original is the default**, and your choice is remembered in that browser on that device.

| Layout | How it presents the plan |
|---|---|
| **Original** | The familiar call-plan panel followed by the formation list. |
| **Quick Call** | The primary call, your job, and quick setup first, with alternatives expandable on demand. |
| **Coaching Board** | The primary call with your job and coaching keys, followed by changeup and pressure sections. Opening an alternative's details expands it across the board and closes the other; collapsing restores the columns. |
| **Formation First** | Planned calls grouped by defensive formation. The primary formation starts open; expand the other groups for their calls, setup, and coaching. |

All four views use the same recommendations. The optional layouts include an expandable **Browse formations** section, and switching layouts preserves your scouting and planning inputs.

The Coaching Board pressure section includes **Setup, your job & coaching**. If a separate pressure call qualifies, its own assignment and setup are shown. Otherwise, the section explains the pressure decision and identifies the **primary call** whose setup and job apply—including when that primary already sends extra rushers.

### Your planned call mix

The plan builds up to four meaningful answers for the current situation: a **Foundation**, proactive coverage **Variation**, a **Counter** to a possible offensive response, and qualifying **Pressure** or another variation. These are choices to mix during a game, not a fixed repeating sequence. Pressure is conditional; it is never added just to fill a tile.

Original uses a compact **2×2 play matrix**; Quick Call puts the foundation first, Coaching Board adds short coaching cues, and Formation First groups the mix by formation. Tap a play to open its full coaching, adjustments, tradeoffs and audible checks; return to the mix with the back button. The existing formation browser remains available.

Instructions identical across every call appear once in **Applies to all calls**, below the plays and collapsed by default. Only the common settings and responsibilities are shared; each call retains its differences. Changing the scout or situation recomputes the shared section. Against tempo, alternatives stay in the same defensive formation; confirm available audibles before the game.

**Test This Call** carries the selected planned call and its setup into the observation form, including when you choose a changeup or pressure option.

### Personalized recommendations

**Best Overall** identifies the strongest matchup for the current inputs. **Best For You** also considers the position you control and your preferred style: Stay Balanced, Protect Explosives, or Create Pressure.

Call details explain when to use the call, what it takes away, what to be ready for, and when to change it. Adjustment guidance includes the tradeoff so you can make a deliberate change as the offense adapts.

### Macros and Play Art

These are two different tools:

- **Macros** answers an offensive problem with a package of adjustments you can reference during a game.
- **Play Art** lets you design and inspect the assignments and alignment of a specific defensive setup.

The app provides instructions and diagrams. Enter the settings yourself in CFB 27; it does not send commands or macros to your game.

## Play Art — Beta

Choose **Family → Formation**, then tap a defender to open that position’s assignment menu.

- **Custom canvases for 71 formations.** Start from the formation’s defenders and choose their assignments.
- **Example calls in four formations:** Nickel Over, 4-3 Over Wide, 3-4 Over, and 3-3-5 Stack. The example library does not include every catalogued play.
- **Position-specific assignments:** supported zones, rushes, blitzes, QB spies, and contain responsibilities.
- **Zone drops:** Default or 0–30 yards in 5-yard increments for flats, curl flats, and hooks.
- **Lane-aware coverage bubbles:** flatter, responsive ovals distinguish deep halves, thirds, quarters, and underneath areas. Crowded underneath symbols retain at least 95% of their original width and height and spread within their assigned lane in the defenders' original left-to-right order. Explicit deep areas and numeric drop depths stay fixed; neighboring areas may still overlap intentionally. Placement is schematic, not a measured coverage boundary.
- **Alignment controls:** corner and safety depth/width, linebacker shifts, and Show Blitz. Secondary Show Blitz brings safeties to 6 yards while keeping corners in place.
- **Defensive line controls:** technique, point of attack, compatible stunts, and left/right/both-edge QB contain.
- **Before / After comparison** on the same field. New Play Art sessions start with an empty Custom setup; the play picker and Step through controls have been removed. Before shows the starting canvas or an explicitly loaded saved macro’s base; After is your editable macro. Reset clears to Custom. Saved macros remain available to load or apply to the current formation.
- **Show changes** is off by default, including when reopening saved macros. Enable it to highlight affected defenders, with dashed original positions and assignment paths where they differ; assignment colors keep their existing meaning. A collapsed change list explains alignment, zone-drop, individual-assignment, and rush-plan differences.
- **Apply a saved macro to the current formation:** each saved macro has separate **Load** and **Apply here** buttons. Load restores its original formation and full setup. Apply keeps the open formation and play, transferring edited assignments to matching position labels. SLB ↔ SLB1, DT ↔ DT1, and SLCB ↔ SLCB1 are supported aliases in both directions. Exact labels take priority; each source maps to at most one target. Second/third slots remain distinct, and SLB never maps to MIKE. These aliases also apply to shared-control and stunt/contain compatibility checks. Unedited stock assignments and unmatched positions remain unchanged. Shared alignment/drop controls are skipped if they would affect unmatched players; stunts and contain require compatible matching participants. A summary lists applied and skipped adjustments. New macro names are limited to 20 characters. Saved rows stay on one line; longer existing names remain saved and display with an ellipsis when needed. The saved original is unchanged; save separately to keep the transferred version. Show changes compares the result against the current play's starting setup.
- **Assignments / Pre-snap views**, reset, and named saved macros. Before previews are read-only; select After to edit. Saving during a preview always saves the complete macro. Comparison is reconstructed for saved macros and every supported formation without changing the storage format.

When a stunt and contain setting need the same defender, the latest selection takes priority. Unavailable stunts explain why they cannot be used.

Comparison steps explain the final settings, not your edit history or a required game-button sequence. Related stunt/contain settings and their rush-assignment changes are previewed together. All comparison modes share the same field scale so depth changes remain directly comparable.

Bubble proportions and spacing were visually reviewed against CFB Labs' published CFB 27 [Nickel Over Cover 3 Sky](https://www.cfblabs.com/plays/NICKEL/OVER/COVER%203%20SKY), [Tampa 2](https://www.cfblabs.com/plays/NICKEL/OVER/TAMPA%202), and [Cover 4 Quarters](https://www.cfblabs.com/plays/NICKEL/OVER/COVER%204%20QUARTERS) art. [Civil's macro guide](https://www.civil.gg/tips/best-defensive-macros-cfb-27) provides context for layered flat/curl-flat drops; separating display symbols does not alter those settings or create new assignments.

Man coverage is a label in the diagram; select the receiver matchup in game. Paths illustrate responsibilities and rush lanes rather than exact animation, timing, or guaranteed pressure. Saved Play Art setups do not change the recommendation engine.

**Report a bug** in Play Art opens an email to [help@schemebuilders.com](mailto:help@schemebuilders.com) with the current setup included.

## How recommendations work

The app uses a deterministic, data-driven football engine running in your browser. Its catalog contains **1,245 defensive plays across 71 formations** and **31 defensive playbooks**.

The recommendation pipeline combines:

- Scouted offensive threats, personnel, and run/pass tendency.
- Your selected defensive playbook and the live down/distance.
- Exact play names and documented assignment evidence.
- Coverage matchup, run-support considerations, and supported adjustment tradeoffs.
- Your defensive user position, preferred style, and game objective.
- The purpose of the exact down and distance, including early-down balance, second-and-short shot risk, and protecting the line to gain on conversion downs.

After evaluating calls, the engine builds a planned mix from verified, available stock plays. Coverage assignments must differ meaningfully; changing formation names alone is not variation. It considers hypothetical offensive responses to repeated coverage, then selects alternatives within bounded fit and threat tradeoffs. It preserves objective, personnel, tempo and existing QB-control safeguards. These responses are anticipated possibilities, not observed tendencies or predictions of the next snap. Scores remain unchanged. Limited playbooks may yield fewer than four calls.

The shared recommendation service supplies the live plan, call details, share text, and PDF call-sheet recommendations.

**Fit scores are heuristic rankings, not success probabilities.** Assignment counts describe call structure; they do not establish exact defender identities, run-gap ownership, or gameplay outcomes. Team profiles are starting points that should be updated from your observations.

**Test This Call records real gameplay observations.** It does not simulate a snap or automatically retrain the engine.

## Phone use and saved data

On iPhone, open the app in Safari and use **Share → Add to Home Screen**. On supported Android and desktop browsers, use **Install** or **Add to Home Screen**.

The app supports light and dark themes. The far-right navigation button changes the theme.

Saved profiles, notes, call tests, and macros stay in the browser on the device where you created them. They do not automatically sync between devices. Use **Export / Import** to back up opponent profiles; those exports do not include Play Art macros.

The call sheet offers **Save PDF**, **View PDF**, and **Edit call sheet** without leaving your plan. Filter by down or show only edited situations; reset an individual situation without losing other edits. In the editor, promote an existing eligible call to the starting slot or exclude alternatives for a specific situation. A summary counts included calls and edited situations. Coaching can be hidden; setup and safety reminders remain. Use **Update PDF** after editing; Save/View stay disabled until the new PDF is ready. Edits apply to the current export only and reset when a new sheet is built. Entirely excluded rows are labeled explicitly; scores and the live plan are unchanged. On iPhone and supported iPads, Save PDF opens the share sheet; choose **Save to Files**. On a desktop, Save PDF starts a direct download.

The PDF is a **two-page quick reference**. Page one lists all twelve down/distance situations with a starting call and eligible variations, counters and pressure options. Page two collects shared setup and user responsibilities once, keyed to each call. Full watch-outs, adjustment reasons and tradeoffs remain in the app; unusually large instruction sets are explicitly marked **APP**. PDF recommendations use the same engine as the live plan, including situational conversion-window costs and second-and-short shot protection. The starting column is part of a mix, not an every-snap prescription.

## Run locally

### Requirements

- Git
- **Node.js 22.13 or newer in the Node 22 release line**, with npm. GitHub Actions uses Node 22.

From a terminal:

```bash
git clone https://github.com/ckeysvt88/schemebuilderv2.git
cd schemebuilderv2
npm ci
npm run dev
```

Open the local URL printed by Vite. To check the interface from a phone on the same Wi-Fi:

```bash
npm run dev -- --host 0.0.0.0
```

Open Vite’s Network URL on the phone. Use HTTPS for installed-app and secure browser features.

To review the production build on a phone on the same Wi-Fi:

```bash
npm run build
npm run preview -- --host 0.0.0.0 --port 4173
```

Keep the terminal open and enter its **Network** URL in the phone's browser. Local hosting has its own saved data, separate from the live site's profiles and notes.

### Situational recommendation rules

All teams and custom scouts use the same engine in the app and PDF. Short conversion downs apply explicit short-yardage formation suitability and warnings. Long conversion downs reduce purely run-based formation penalties while retaining personnel, quick-pass and tempo warnings; explicit run-heavy scouting limits this reduction. Equal formation scores use the conversion situation's purpose before alphabetical order.

Eligible long-yardage menus can include a **Pressure look**: a documented mugged formation with four or fewer stock rushers and deep help. This is distinct from an extra-rusher blitz and does not promise a successful disguise or verified simulated-pressure exchange. **No Quick TD** retains at least two deep defenders and limits stock rushers to four for primary calls and alternatives, including personalized choices.

Regression coverage includes every saved team across twelve call-sheet situations and every personnel-family/playbook pairing with varied tendencies, objectives and preferences. This is broad coverage, not an exhaustive test of every possible input combination.

### Personnel fit and balanced situational plans

Selecting a preset team resets the opponent tendency to **Balanced**. Presets describe concepts and available formations, not measured run/pass frequency; change the tendency to match your actual opponent.

Recommendations now assess the current offensive personnel against defensive-back counts from the formation alignment. Three- and four-receiver looks increase the cost of relying on base personnel, especially with a pass-heavy tendency or long conversion yardage. Short conversion downs reduce that cost, and light packages retain a run-exposure cost against heavier personnel. These are provisional package-level judgments; player speed, actual substitutions and individual coverage matchups are not known. Grizzly is categorized as **Pressure**, not Pass Defense; each call's actual rush count still determines whether it sends extra rushers.

For down/distance recommendations, the shared engine reviews the twelve situational rows together. It can choose an eligible personalized foundation within **four fit points** of the isolated best to reduce repeated calls and coverage structures. Scores remain unchanged. The planned foundation appears first, with other formations in fit order; its expanded details explain when plan variety influenced the selection. This is a deterministic planning mix, not a live play history, random rotation, or fixed sequence. Restricted inventories may still require repetition. Exact distances are checked again against the same safeguards.

Between-snap package alternatives can cross Base/Nickel/Dime categories when their personnel fit qualifies. Tempo still keeps alternatives in one formation; every formation change requires checking substitutions and available audibles. The mix retains the original best call's safeguards as well as the chosen foundation's safeguards. The app, sharing and the two-page PDF use the same selection logic.

Football context: [USA Football — defending 20 personnel](https://blogs.usafootball.com/blog/7188/learn-how-to-defend-the-20-personnel-run-game) and [X&O Labs — defending the spread with quarters](https://www.xandolabs.com/the-lab/defense/coverage/two-high-coverage-structures/defending-the-spread-with-quarters-coverage/) explain the hybrid/slot responsibilities behind personnel matching. They do not validate this app's numeric weights or CFB gameplay results.

### Formation comparison and playbook finder

- **Forms → Compare formations** overlays two catalog alignments on the same field. Each side uses **Family → Formation** selectors to keep the formation list focused. On a formation detail, **Compare** sits beside **Customize** and starts with that formation selected. Translucent red and blue circles with thin borders distinguish the two formations; a split-color circle identifies a shared starting location; hide either layer, tap a marker, or enable position labels. These are schematic starting alignments, not coverage assignments.
- **Compare → Find my playbook** lets you search or filter by family, assign **Must**/**Nice** directly, or select multiple rows and set priorities in bulk. **Select shown** targets the current filtered list; **Show prioritized only** makes review faster. Priorities are remembered on this browser; Undo restores the last selection change. Search accepts punctuation-free names (for example 335) and comma-separated alternatives. Books meeting every must-have rank by nice-to-have coverage. If none qualify, closest alternatives explicitly list missing requirements. Included/missing lists are collapsed to keep large searches compact. Start with three results and expand for more. Open a result in playbook comparison or view its schools. Matching uses the current catalog, treats distinct formation names separately, and excludes the “All” aggregate.

### Animated match responsibilities

**Play Art → Match Responsibilities · Beta** (also accessible from Forms) opens a focused, interactive teaching field from the formation list or a formation detail. Select the called coverage, offensive look, available check, and receiver releases. Play, pause, or replay at a fixed **2× speed**, or scrub to any point using the high-contrast timeline (visible handle, progress fill, percentage and keyboard support). Stage buttons jump to alignment, first steps, or response; choosing another check or release resets playback. Follow a defensive role, flip the field, or expand all responsibilities. Reduced-motion preferences disable automatic playback while preserving scrubbing and stage selection.

The field includes a center reference, ball marker and separate line of scrimmage. Solid defender-to-receiver links identify assigned matches; dashed links identify reads or bracket help. The inside-underneath role (H, often a linebacker) remains visible in zone-based stack and spread examples. Unconfirmed short-inside pickups are shown as reads, not invented man assignments. Zone areas and deep-help/read landmarks identify their owner and fade with the timeline; they are schematic responsibilities, not measured coverage boundaries.

Menus and defaults now follow [The Gaming Tailgate CFB27 adjustment guide](https://www.thegamingtailgate.com/CFB27DefensiveCoachAdjustmentsNew.pdf), pages 5–7. There are 17 named concepts. Stump is included for Palms/Cover 6 trips; Cover 3 stack uses Seam; Cover 6 stack also offers Palms. Top Hat and Triangle variants are excluded pending CFB-specific confirmation. Man-bunch defaults to Lock. Base 2×2 remains a teaching context, not a separate game menu. Tight/wide receiver spacing remains distinct.

The movement engine and captions share receiver assignments and responsibility modes. New release examples exercise short-inside #2/#3 and short-outside #1. Documented linebacker pickups stay attached to their receivers; Solo Cut falls back to Solo on the non-cut example; Stump's explicitly short #1 example releases C/N into space. Conditional zone areas fade in together, while declared matches retain receiver links without bubbles. Crowded or unspecified exchanges remain reads. Stump's approximate trigger is not a calibrated pixel-to-yard test.

Owner-confirmed follow-up: **“It's not where you line up, it's where you wind up.”** Eligible match responsibilities now distribute by the receivers' releases while preserving fixed Lock, MEG and point-lock rules. Try **RB flat / #2 drag**: the RB starts as #3, takes the outside release, and goes to the flat defender; the original #2 finishes inside and is picked up by H. Original receiver IDs remain stable so the exchange is traceable. Stump returns to Stubbie when its short-#1 trigger is absent; Skinny carries the inside drag; Box all-vertical examples use C→1, S→2 and H→3. Hook Curl, Vertical Hook and 3 Rec Hook descriptions explain the #3 match responsibility. Numeric **Hooks** drops affect those three assignments; **Middle Read**, a Cover 2 responsibility, retains its own depth.

These remain schematic teaching animations, not verified in-game simulations or exact personnel assignments. Playback does not edit your plan or saved macro. The accompanying planner corrections remove unsupported stack-check recommendations, use the available 9-yard CB depth instead of 10, and clarify Tight CB width against compressed receivers. Macro recipes use that same valid CB depth. Numeric fit-score weights are unchanged: the guide supplies no measured scoring calibration. See the [Tailgate audit and review flags](docs/TAILGATE_MATCH_AUDIT.md), including differences from [EA's broader gameplay description](https://www.ea.com/games/ea-sports-college-football/college-football-27/news/college-football-27-gameplay).

The call-sheet export panel keeps **Save PDF**, **View PDF**, and **Edit call sheet** together. Editing expands within that panel; update the PDF after editing before viewing or saving. Playbook Finder upgrades the existing Compare workflow.

Playbook Finder priority rows reserve space inside the scrolling list so both Must/Nice button borders remain visible on narrow screens.

Match Responsibilities maintenance: see the [accuracy and UI audit](docs/MATCH_RESPONSIBILITIES_AUDIT.md) for corrected release conditions, alignment checks, source limits, and verification scope.
